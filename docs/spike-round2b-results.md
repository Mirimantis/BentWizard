## Read this first: headless cannot adjudicate the round-2 failure

Before building anything, this harness tried to reproduce round 2's
`Tool shape is null` **on the exact artifact that failed**,
`scratch/r2_cut_timber.FCStd`, opened headless in FreeCAD 1.1.1:

- `JointCut` reports `Up-to-date` / `Valid`, before and after a
  recompute.
- Changing `JointParams.MortiseThickness` to 4 in updates the cut
  to 98 682 899.408 mm³ — exactly timber minus a 4 in mortise
  cutter.
- No object in the document is left touched or errored.

The same sequence built from scratch — copy an `App::Part`, put it
in a `PartDesign::Boolean`, change a parameter, save, close,
reopen, change again — is also clean headlessly.

**So the brief's premise needs one correction.** The round-2
harness was genuinely deficient: it asserted a volume and never an
object state, and that is fixed here and retrofitted into rounds 1
and 2. But the new assertion would *not* have caught this bug,
because headless FreeCAD does not exhibit it. `Tool shape is null`
is a GUI-side recompute-ordering failure, and no headless harness
can be trusted to see it.

That does not rescue C1-Part. A bridge that works headlessly and
fails in the GUI is a broken bridge, and the diagnosis in the
brief is sound on its own terms: `App::Part` is a container, not a
shape-bearing object, and `PartDesign::Boolean` documents Bodies as
its operands. Round 2b removes the container and uses the operand
FreeCAD actually expects. The decisive evidence was always GUI
inspection of `scratch/r2b_cut_timber.FCStd`, not this report --
**and that inspection has since been done and passed. See *GUI
confirmation* below.**

## Does removing the container fix it?

Headlessly, `C1` passes every criterion, including the three
that round 2 never checked: after each parameter change no
object is left touched or errored, and the console stays
silent. It also honours the instance `Placement` and leaves the
timber's own section sketch driving it.

The structural argument for believing this over round 2:

- The operand is a `PartDesign::Body`, which is what
  `PartDesign::Boolean` is documented to take. Round 2 handed
  it an `App::Part`, which carries no shape of its own.
- **No `GeoFeatureGroup` error can arise**, because there is no
  container for the Body to already belong to. In round 2 that
  error was what ruled out the Body-as-operand form and forced
  the Part-as-operand form that failed.
- `copyObject(body, True)` pulls the VarSet in as a dependency
  on its own, so the container bought nothing in the first
  place. It existed only because round 1's Link could not see
  a child VarSet.

## GUI confirmation (2026-08-30)

Adam opened `scratch/r2b_cut_timber.FCStd` in the FreeCAD GUI and
exercised the path that failed in round 2. Reported:

- **It works.** Changes to the instance's `JointParams` VarSet
  update the geometry **immediately and without problems** -- no
  `Tool shape is null`, no stale cut, no forced refresh.
- **Placing the housed mortise on different faces and stations is
  a simple transform**, and looks straightforward to automate.

That settles the question round 2b existed to answer. The
container-free template with bridge `C1` is confirmed in the only
environment that could adjudicate it, and the withdrawal of round
2's C1-Part is confirmed as a container problem rather than a
problem with `copyObject`.

The second observation is worth keeping, because it contradicts a
worry inherited from `apply_joint.py`. Round 1 surfaced two
placement bugs -- a mirrored sketch needing its `Angle`
constraints negated, and a frame-child's `AttachmentOffset`
negating on an axis that depends on the plane it attaches to --
and both are artifacts of *rebuilding sketches on the target*.
Positioning a copied solid is a rigid transform of one object, so
neither can arise. One caveat survives: a rigid transform cannot
produce a *mirrored* solid, so a handed joint still needs either a
mirrored cutter or the existing `Template_Handed` flag. Faces,
ends and stations are transforms; hand is not.

## Does it close the performance gap?

At 150 instances:

- full recompute **5.80 s**, against round 2's 8.30 s and
  round 1's 2.76 s;
- build **28.1 s**, against 44.0 s and 12.2 s;
- **4051 objects**, against 5401 and 4351.

**The gap barely narrows, and the brief's explanation for
it is wrong.** Removing the container drops 25% of the
objects — the `App::Part` and one `App::Origin` per
instance — but recovers only 30% of the recompute time.
So the extra `App::Origin` was **not** the cause of round
2's penalty: object count and recompute cost are not
proportional here, and `copyObject` remains roughly
2.1x round 1's recompute at this scale.

What is left is the cost a deep copy cannot avoid:
duplicating and re-solving the cutter's whole feature tree
— two sketches and two pads per instance — where a variant
link's materialized copy is evidently cheaper to refresh.
That is a real and permanent tax on this mechanism, and it
should be weighed as such rather than expected to
disappear. It does not change the recommendation: 5.8 s
for a 150-joint frame is fine, and growth is linear.

## What the round-2 harness should have asserted

Round 2's T3 asserted, after a parameter change, only that the
volume reached its analytic expectation. A model can pass that
while carrying a failed feature — and, in the GUI, round 2's did.
The assertion is now three-part, in
`spike_report.changed_cleanly`:

1. the measured value reaches the analytic expectation;
2. **no object is left outside `Up-to-date`** — every object's
   `State` and `getStatusString()` is enumerated, and anything
   else is a FAIL;
3. **the console is silent** during the recompute — captured at
   the file-descriptor level, since FreeCAD warns from C++;
   `shape is null`, `out of the allowed scope`, `hasher mismatch`
   and friends are all faults.

It is applied at **every** parameter change in this harness, and
`spike_report.unhealthy()` / `console_faults()` are now used by the
round 1 and round 2 harnesses too, so all three stay comparable
and the regression suite is uniform. Rerunning rounds 1 and 2 with
the assertion in place leaves both still passing headlessly —
which is the point above: headless is not where this bug lives.

## Recommendation

**Template structure: container-free.** A `JointParams` VarSet
and a `Cutter` Body at document root, no `App::Part`. This is
the Phase 0 pattern, it is what a template author would build
without being told otherwise, and it is fewer objects.

**Bridge: `C1` — PartDesign::Boolean with the copied Body in Group.**

`C3` also passes, producing a `Part::Cut`; `C1` is
preferred because it keeps the timber a
`PartDesign::Body`, so nothing downstream changes.

**The GUI condition on this recommendation has been met.**
Round 2 also passed every headless assertion and was wrong, so
adopting round 2b was made conditional on a GUI session with
`scratch/r2b_cut_timber.FCStd`. That session happened
(2026-08-30) and the cut follows its parameters immediately.
Round 2b therefore stands on evidence, not on headless
inference, and round 2 is superseded in its template structure
and bridge only — the `copyObject`-over-links conclusion rested
on authoring ergonomics, not on T3, and is unaffected.

Everything round 2 listed as still owed by the apply-joint
tool — mated pairs, mate frames and the stick-allowance contract,
placement computation, naming and filing, assembly seating —
is unchanged, with one item downgraded: placement is now a
rigid transform of a copied solid rather than a sketch rebuild
on the target, which is a materially smaller job than
`apply_joint.py` does today (see *GUI confirmation*).
