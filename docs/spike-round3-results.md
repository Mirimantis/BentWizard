## Verdict on Risk 1 -- coplanar-face fusion

**The coincidence itself is a non-issue.** A tenon shoulder
exactly coplanar with the stick's end face fuses to one valid,
closed solid with *bit-identical* topology to the 1 mm-overlap
case -- 11 faces, 24 edges, 16 vertexes, the same volume to
full precision -- and the console stays silent. This is not the
island-pocket failure mode repeating: a pocket's coincident
loops are a *sketch* problem, and there is no sketch here.

**But the risk was in the wrong place, and the real one is
worse.** Three separate ways of getting this wrong all produce
*exactly the expected volume*, pass `isValid()` and
`isClosed()`, and leave every object `Up-to-date`:

1. **A 1 mm gap** between adder and end face -- the R1 control.
   Two solids, correct volume.
2. **A cutter whose footprint spans the section** -- the T4
   control. It removes exactly the intended volume *and cuts
   the timber in half*.
3. **A seat computed in the wrong frame** -- see below. The
   adder lands clean off the end of the stick, and the volume
   is still right to the last digit.

Only **solid count** catches any of them. That is the single
most important thing round 3 has to say: under a cutter+adder
model, `len(shape.Solids) == 1` is not a nicety, it is the
assertion that distinguishes a joint from a severed timber.

### The operand-frame finding

`PartDesign::Boolean` seats its operand in the **target Body's
local frame**; the target's own `Placement` is not applied. So
a joint half is placed at the timber's *local* end, and adding
the timber's own offset -- the obvious thing to do -- moves the
adder off the end face by exactly that offset. The harness made
this mistake while being written, and the only symptom was the
solid count. This is the same family as Phase 0 finding #10:
resolve the frame before believing a coordinate.

## Verdict on Risk 2 -- derived length

**Sound, with one condition made explicit.** Derived length is
exact -- 0.000000 mm error in all four cases against the
brief's 0.001 mm tolerance -- for a square stick, one tenon,
two tenons, and a 30-degree end.

The condition is the frame. The test timber is deliberately
rotated 37 degrees; its *global* bounding box reads 2069.68 mm
against a true 2438.40 mm. Resolving
`shape.Placement = body.Placement.inverse() * shape.Placement`
first makes the measurement exact. A cut list that reads a
global box will short every rotated timber, silently.

**Long point versus short point.** For the 30-degree end the
long point is 96.000 in and the short point 91.381 in. The cut
list must order the **long point**: a sawyer makes the angle by
cutting it, and cannot cut an angled end onto a stick already
cut to the short point -- ordering the short point yields every
brace 4.619 in shy. Conveniently the local bounding box *is*
the long point, so the naive measurement is the right one; the
short point needs the sawn face, found as the one face whose
normal is not axis-aligned.

Design length and order length genuinely diverge: the two-tenon
stick is 96 in of design and 104 in of timber. That is the
claimed benefit, and it holds.

## Joint swapping, quantified (T3)

Swapping a tenon half for a housed-mortise half on a placed,
rotated timber moved nothing: `Placement` identical, design length
identical, section sketch untouched, both bearing faces moved
0.000000000 mm. The cost is 13 deletions and 15 creations -- one
`copyObject` and one Boolean either side.

The comparison that matters is not the object count but what is
*not* touched. Under the subtractive mandate the same swap changes
the tenon length, which changes the ordered stick length, which
moves the stick's ends, which moves every assembly joint grounded
off them. Here the design solid is inert and only the attached
components change.

## Both halves, one mechanism (T4)

A mortise (cutter) and a tenon (adder) driven from one
`J-HousedMT-001` VarSet stay matched through parameter changes, and
the fit is *measured from the solids* -- a section taken clear of
the housing -- not re-read from the parameters. Intended 1/32 in,
measured 0.031250 in on both thickness and width; changed to
1/16 in, measured 1/16 in. Both halves are `PartDesign::Boolean`
over a copied solid, differing only in `Type`. No island pocket
appears anywhere, which is claimed benefit 1, confirmed.

## Boolean order is NOT commutative (T5)

The brace half -- an angled shoulder cutter plus a tenon adder,
one VarSet -- gives **different solids** depending on order:

- cut-then-add: 10 faces, volume 98365613.7 mm³
- add-then-cut: 6 faces, volume 98260085.8 mm³

Six faces is a plain angled stick: applied second, the shoulder
cutter sawed the tenon off along with the waste. Both results
are single valid closed solids, so **nothing but the face count
and volume distinguishes a correct brace from a brace with no
tenon**.

**Order therefore belongs in the template contract.** The
recommendation is *cutter first, then adder*: a cutter shapes
the stick the adder lands on, which is also the order a framer
works in. A template must not be free to leave this implicit.

## Template validation sweep (T6b)

360 template solids were built and asserted across 4 templates in
6.23 s -- about 17 ms per step, roughly 1.6 s per template.

**The sweep found a real hole, silently.** In the mortise
cutter, `HousingHeight` below about 1.7 in leaves the housing
pad no longer overlapping the mortise prism (which starts at
`SetbackFace1` = 2 in), so the template's own solid becomes
**two disjoint solids** -- with no exception, no console
output, `isValid()` and `isClosed()` both true, and every
object `Up-to-date`. A joint applied from that template would
cut two unrelated pockets.

Concretely:

- `mortise cutter` at `HousingHeight` = 1.0000 in: 2 solids, valid=True, raised=None, silent=True
- `mortise cutter` at `HousingHeight` = 1.6364 in: 2 solids, valid=True, raised=None, silent=True

This is finding #14 **relocated, not removed**, exactly as the
brief predicted -- and the relocation is an improvement, because
authoring time is a place a tool can check.

## Head-to-head against the subtractive model (T7)

Same physical timber, built both ways:

| | subtractive (island pocket) | adder |
|---|---|---|
| objects in the document | 15 | 25 |
| objects the joint contributes | 2 | 13 |
| objects in its template | n/a | 12 |
| full recompute | 0.0250 s | 0.0244 s |
| resulting solid | 1 solids, 11 faces | 1 solids, 11 faces |
| tenon-thickness sweep 0.5-4.0 in | no failures | no failures |

**Stated as plainly as the numbers allow: it is a dead heat on
geometry and a loss on object count.** Both models produce the
identical solid -- same volume to full precision, same 11
faces -- and both survive the full sweep with the three-part
assertion at every step. The adder model costs 13 objects per
joint against the subtractive model's 2, because a copied
template body brings its own sketches, pads and origin where a
pocket is two objects inside the timber. Recompute is a wash
(0.0244 s vs 0.0250 s at one joint).

So **the adder model fails nowhere the subtractive model does
not** -- but it does not win on the geometry either. The case
for it is entirely the architectural one: one mechanism instead
of two, and an inert design solid that joint swaps do not
disturb.

## Scale

At 150 instances (alternating cutters and adders) round 3 builds
in 45.7 s and full-recomputes in 5.5 s, against round 2b's
28.1 s and 5.8 s for cutters alone.
**A fusion does not cost more than a cut** at this scale.
Every timber in the 150-instance document was checked for
severing, not just for volume.

## Recommendation

**Adopt cutter + adder, with conditions.** Both risks are
cleared, both claimed benefits hold, and the head-to-head shows
no geometric regression. The conditions are not optional
polish -- each one is something this spike caught only because
it was looking:

1. **Solid count is a mandatory post-condition.** After every
   boolean, `len(shape.Solids) == 1` (for a timber that was one
   solid before). Three distinct errors in this spike produced
   exactly-correct volumes with wrong geometry, and volume,
   `isValid()`, `isClosed()` and object state were blind to all
   three. This belongs in the linter as a strict rule and in
   apply-joint as a refusal.
2. **Boolean order is part of the template contract.** Cutter
   before adder, declared, not inferred. T5 shows the two
   orders give different solids, both valid.
3. **Seats are computed in the target Body's local frame.**
   Not global. Document it where `apply_joint` computes
   placements.
4. **A parameter sweep is mandatory at template
   registration.** See below.

What this does *not* settle: the subtractive mandate is a
governing rule with a stated purpose -- that `Length` means the
ordered stick. Cutter+adder changes that contract to *design
length is the frame dimension, order length is derived*. That
is a roadmap decision, not a geometry one, and this spike
supports it rather than making it.

## What the apply-joint tool must now do

1. **Read an explicit role per half.** A half declares `Cutter`,
   `Adder`, or both -- it cannot be inferred, because a butt joint
   has neither and a brace has both. The natural home is a Tier-2
   property on each template body, in the same spirit as
   `Frame_Role`: a declared role is never guessed at.
2. **Apply in a declared order.** Cutter first, then adder, with
   the order named in the template rather than implied by object
   creation sequence.
3. **Assert the post-condition.** One solid, after each boolean,
   with the failure reported against the joint that caused it.
4. **Derive order length instead of storing it.** From the local
   bounding box, with the sawn-face long/short point where an end
   is not square, and the long point going to the cut list.
5. **Seat halves in the target's local frame**, which is also what
   makes a swap free: the timber never moves.
6. Everything rounds 1-2b already listed -- mated pairs, mate
   frames and the stick-allowance contract, naming and filing,
   assembly seating -- is unchanged. Note that the
   stick-allowance contract needs rethinking under a derived
   length: `Stick_Allowance_FTF` exists to reconcile a typed
   `Length` with joinery, and a derived length removes the thing
   it was reconciling.

## Template registration validation

**Make the sweep mandatory at registration.** It costs about
1.6 s per template here -- well inside what Save as Joint Template
already spends -- and in this run it found a silent two-solid hole
that no amount of inspection at default values would reveal.

Concretely:

- **Templates must declare per-property valid ranges.** The sweep
  needs a domain, and 25%-150% of the default is a guess. A
  declared range is also the thing the apply dialog should clamp
  to, so it earns its keep twice.
- **The roadmap's sanity bounds become the sweep domain**, not
  advisory guidance: mortise width <= 75% of the receiving extent,
  housing <= 50% of the through-dimension, dovetail flare clamped
  to the member extent. Those are already written down as the
  values a joint stops making sense outside of; sweeping them is
  what turns them from advice into a check.
- **The assertion at each step is topological**: one solid, valid,
  closed, nothing left touched, console silent. Not volume.
- **Report, do not block** -- consistent with how
  `template_check.py` already treats completeness. An author may
  have a good reason for a narrow domain; they should not be able
  to miss that they have one.
